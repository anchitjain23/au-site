Thinking

Every finance app in existence was built to show you the past. We thought that was insane. So we built something different. This is the honest comparison nobody else will write — what every major app actually does, what it costs, and why Au exists.

Every app tells you the score after the game is over. Au tells you what the next move costs you before you make it.

Switching from
Monarch

Monarch calls itself an AI-powered finance app. In our view, their AI is effectively a chat window sitting on top of data it was already displaying. Ask it what you're about to spend is going to cost you toward your goals and it goes completely silent — because Monarch was never built to operate before a decision. Only after.

That is not AI. That is search with a friendlier font. And for that, they charge up to $14.99 a month.

There is no wishlist. No Goal Impact Bar. No concept of what a specific decision costs you in time toward the thing you are actually working toward. No moment where Monarch steps in before a purchase and gives you real information that changes what you do next. It knows your past with reasonable accuracy. It has zero opinion about your future. That is not financial intelligence. That is a receipt with a subscription attached.

Atom runs on your complete financial picture simultaneously: your goals, your spending patterns, your income, your net worth, your wishlist, your upcoming bills — all of it, in real time, every time you ask it anything. When you're standing in a store about to spend $600 on something, Atom tells you exactly what that costs you in days toward your house deposit before you tap pay. Monarch tells you about it next month.

Monarch shows you where your money went. Au changes where it goes next.

Switching from
YNAB

YNAB charges up to $14.99 a month — and then requires you to take courses to use it. They have built an entire education business around the fact that their product is too complicated to use without instruction. That is not depth. That is a confession.

Most people quit before the free trial ends and blame themselves. There is no AI. There is no wishlist intelligence. There is no concept of what a decision costs you in time. It is a budgeting ideology with a subscription fee attached.

Atom knows your goals, your spending, and your entire financial picture in real time — and tells you exactly what every decision costs you in days before you make it. No courses. No methodology to memorise. No homework required.

YNAB asks you to change how you think about money. Au just shows you what your money is actually doing.

Switching from
Rocket Money

Rocket Money built its business on one real insight: people overpay on bills and hate dealing with it. The bill negotiation feature works. We'll give them that.

But Rocket Money charges a monthly subscription for premium features, and on top of that takes a percentage cut of whatever they save you through bill negotiation. The savings they show you and the savings you actually keep are two different numbers. Read their pricing carefully before you sign up.

Au includes bill negotiation at no extra cost and takes no cut of what you save. Atom generates a personalised negotiation script built around your specific provider, your tenure as a customer, and current market rates. Every dollar you save is yours — and bill negotiation is one feature inside a product that also has real AI, a Goal Impact Bar, wishlist intelligence, household finance, and a philosophy built around the moment before you spend.

Rocket Money profits when you save. Au just helps you save.

Switching from
Cleo

Cleo is a gimmick. A well-executed one, but a gimmick nonetheless. It roasts you for your spending, sends you memes about your bank balance, and calls that financial intelligence. Being told you're broke in a funny voice does not make you less broke. It just makes the experience of being broke more entertaining.

There is no real AI. No goals. No wishlist. No concept of what a specific decision costs you in time toward anything that actually matters to you. It knows your last transaction and has a joke ready about it. That is not intelligence.

Atom knows what Cleo never bothered to build — your goals, your wishlist, your net worth, your upcoming bills, every pattern across every category since day one. After 30 days it builds your Spending Personality: a sharp, accurate read on how you actually relate to money, designed to be shared because it feels uncomfortably real. Not a roast. An insight.

Cleo makes being bad with money feel fun. Atom makes being good with money feel effortless.

Cleo is a punchline. Atom is the answer.

Switching from
Copilot

In our view, Copilot categorises your past spending with accuracy and calls that AI. Knowing what you spent on last Thursday is not intelligence — it is record keeping. Intelligence is knowing what you are about to spend and what it will cost you before you do it.

And at $13 a month — for a single user, Apple devices only, no Android, no household sharing — that is a significant constraint for a product positioning itself as premium.

Atom operates at the exact moment you are making a financial decision — not after it. It works on iOS and Android, covers your entire household, and runs on your complete financial picture simultaneously. The gap between what Copilot does and what Au does is not a feature difference. It is a fundamental product difference.

Copilot is the best finance app of the last decade. Au is the first finance app of the next one.

Switching from
Mint

Mint had 20 years. 22 million users. And in January 2024 they shut it down and redirected every single one of those users to Credit Karma — a credit score tool with banner ads and zero budgeting capability. That was their answer to two decades of trust. Not a better product. Not a migration path. Just a redirect to something completely different.

It was a guilt machine dressed up as an app. Most users deleted it within a month — not because they stopped caring about money, but because being shown damage after it has already happened is not intelligence. It is a post-mortem. Nobody improves their finances from a post-mortem.

Mint was free because the model depended on advertising, not on serving you. Au contains no ads and no advertiser relationships — and unlike Mint, it operates before the damage is done, not after.

Mint showed you what you spent. Au changes what you spend.

Every app on this page does something. Some do it well. But not one of them operates at the moment that actually matters. You can keep comparing — or try the product that made every other app on this page look like what it is: a very expensive way to look at last month.
